CLINICAL LOGIC AND DATA INTEGRITY JUSTIFICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------

This document outlines the clinical invariants, mathematical checks, 
and data leakage prevention strategies applied during the data 
preprocessing phase (via the `apply_clinical_logic` function).

1. PAIRWISE CONTRADICTIONS (LOGICAL ERRORS)
These checks identify variables that are mutually exclusive or highly
implausible in a valid clinical reality. Depending on the rule, values
are either nullified (set to NaN) to represent irresolvable data-entry 
errors, or flagged for exploratory analysis without data destruction.

  1.1. Weight Invariant (Pre-pregnancy vs. Admission and Gain)
     Clinical logic dictates that a patient's weight at admission is generally 
     expected to be greater than or equal to their pre-pregnancy weight. Although 
     modest weight loss may occur due to clinical conditions such as hyperemesis 
     gravidarum, a reduction exceeding 10 kg is considered clinically implausible 
     and is treated as a data-quality anomaly. Because the recorded weight_gain 
     variable does not fully overlap with its parent weight measurements, the 
     clinical threshold is evaluated independently for both the calculated weight 
     difference (admission minus pre-pregnancy) and the recorded weight_gain value. 
     To preserve valid observations, only the component violating the clinical 
     threshold is nullified. Parent weight variables are nullified when the 
     calculated difference exceeds the threshold, whereas weight_gain is nullified 
     only when its recorded value violates the same criterion.

  1.2. Amniotic Fluid Invariant (Polyhydramnios vs. Oligohydramnios)
       A patient cannot present with both excessive amniotic fluid 
       (polyhydramnios) and insufficient amniotic fluid 
       (oligohydramnios) during a singleton pregnancy at the same 
       point in time. If both flags equal 1, they are set to NaN.

  1.3. Hypertension Type Invariant
       By clinical definition and ICD coding conventions, chronic 
       hypertension (pre-existing) and gestational hypertension 
       (pregnancy-induced) are mutually exclusive primary classifications. 
       However, preeclampsia can superimposed on either condition. Therefore, 
       co-occurrence of chronic and gestational HTN is treated as a 
       contradiction and nullified, while combinations with preeclampsia 
       are preserved as clinically valid.

  1.4. Delivery Pathway Invariant (Planned CS vs. Induction)
       While a planned Cesarean section does not typically involve labor 
       induction, a co-occurrence often represents a "failed induction" 
       where a patient underwent induction that later required an emergency 
       or unplanned CS. To avoid losing this valuable clinical pattern, 
       this rule is treated as a non-destructive audit check (Log Only) 
       to evaluate the volume of failed inductions rather than automatically 
       nullifying the data.

  1.5. Temporal Invariant (Admission vs. Birth Date)
       Chronologically, the admission date must precede or equal the 
       birth date. This is an absolute clinical rule; violations are 
       strictly nullified.

2. DERIVED VARIABLES AND MATHEMATICAL INVARIANTS
These checks ensure that derived features mathematically align with 
their source components based on clinical formulas.

  2.1. HTN Cluster Recalculation (The HTN Cluster)
       The overarching variable 'any_htn' represents the union (OR logic) 
       of its components. To ensure structural truth and protect against 
       source registry mismatches, 'any_htn' is strictly recalculated 
       as the maximum value of 'chronic_htn', 'gestational_htn', and 
       'preeclampsia'.

  2.2. BMI Mathematical Verification
       Rather than verifying and correcting minor rounding errors in the 
       original registry's BMI field, a transparent, standalone feature 
       called 'bmi_pre_pregnancy' is calculated for the entire cohort 
       using the standard formula: Pre-pregnancy Weight / (Height in meters)^2. 
       This provides a clean, uniform predictor based entirely on early 
       pregnancy metrics.

3. DATA LEAKAGE PREVENTION
Variables containing future information (post-prediction-time) are 
strictly isolated from the main model to prevent target leakage.

  3.1. Exclusion of Birth Weight ('birth_weight_g')
       Neonatal birth weight is determined post-delivery and is inherently 
       unavailable at the triage/admission decision point. To prevent data 
       leakage, this column is completely excluded from the modeling phase 
       (handled during feature selection).

  3.2. Note on Meconium and Membrane Color
       While 'meconium' manifests early in labor, it is acknowledged as 
       a borderline data-leakage variable since membrane rupture may 
       occur intrapartum. Per project guidelines, it is excluded from the 
       primary prediction model and reserved strictly for sensitivity 
       analyses.
